THINK FIRST Do you need to find and digitise all 100 clinics manually?
Not necessarily.

If you have usable addresses or place descriptions, GIS can attempt to convert them into geographic locations.

This process is called GEOCODING.
📍 What Is Geocoding?

Geocoding is the process of converting an address, place name or other location description into a geographic location that can be displayed on a map.

In simple terms: text describing a place → map location.

🤔 But Does Every Address Work Perfectly?

No. Geocoding is not simply “address in → perfect point out.”

Different records can produce different results.

✓

MATCHED

A suitable location was found.

?

REVIEW

A possible location was found, but it should be checked.

✕

UNMATCHED

The system could not confidently determine a location.

⚠️ Important: A Match Does Not Automatically Mean Correct

Imagine the spreadsheet contains:

“Jalan Melaka”

Is that description specific enough?

A geocoder may return a result, but you still need to decide whether that result makes sense for your purpose.

🔎 Why Might Geocoding Have Problems?

✏️ Spelling
The address may contain errors.
🧩 Incomplete Address
Important location information may be missing.
📍 Ambiguous Place
Several places may have similar names.
🕒 Outdated Information
Roads or places may have changed.

🔗 Remember Week 4: Can You Trust the Data?

The same quality questions still apply after geocoding.

📍 WHERE?
Is the point in the correct location?
📝 WHAT?
Was the correct clinic matched?
🧩 MISSING?
Did some addresses remain unmatched?
🕒 WHEN?
Is the reference information current?

Never assume that every geocoded point is correct simply because it appears on the map.

💡 What You Should Take Away

Geocoding converts location descriptions such as addresses or place names into geographic locations.

It can be much more efficient than manually creating large numbers of locations.

But a successful match does not automatically mean a correct location. Geocoded results must still be checked.
