🧩 Connecting Data

GIS often needs to bring information from different sources together.

One way this can happen is when two datasets contain a common field or identifier.

That shared information helps GIS connect the correct record from one dataset with the corresponding feature or record in another.

🌐 GIS Data Can Come From Many Sources

The same idea applies beyond our campus example. A GIS project may need to work with information from:

📊 Spreadsheet
CSV or Excel containing names, IDs, coordinates or measurements.
🗺️ Existing GIS Layer
Roads, buildings, boundaries or other existing geographic features.
🌐 Open Data
Data obtained from agencies, portals or other providers.
📍 Field Data
Locations and observations collected directly from the real world.
⚠️ Do Not Rush to Combine Everything

Before bringing datasets together, ask:

Do they really refer to the same things?

Similar-looking data do not automatically belong together. You need a meaningful connection between them.

🧠 Find the Connection

You have:

Dataset A: A GIS layer of campus buildings containing Building_ID.

Dataset B: An energy-use spreadsheet containing Building_ID and monthly electricity consumption.

What is the most useful field for connecting the datasets?

✓ Correct.

Building_ID appears in both datasets and identifies the corresponding building.
Not quite.

Look for a field that appears in both datasets and identifies the same real-world feature.

🔗 But What If the Data Do Not Match?

Suppose one dataset contains:

B01 · B02 · B03

but the second contains:

B01 · B2 · B03

Will everything connect correctly?

🔑 MATCH?
Do the identifiers correspond?
🧩 MISSING?
Are some records absent?
🔄 CONSISTENT?
Are values recorded in the same way?
🕒 CURRENT?
Do the datasets represent compatible time periods?

This is why data often need to be prepared before they are ready for GIS.

💡 What You Should Take Away

GIS projects often use information from multiple sources.

A spatial layer may tell us WHERE something is, while another table provides additional information about WHAT it is like.

A meaningful common field or identifier can help connect those pieces of information.

But before using the combined data, we need to check that the values are complete, consistent and suitable.
