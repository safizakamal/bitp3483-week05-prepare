Digitising & Feature Creation

LEARN THE CONCEPT

From the real world to a GIS feature

In 02 DISCOVER, you found that real-world information can enter GIS in several ways.

Now we will focus on one important method: digitising.

📘 Connect to Reading 01

Your reading introduced an important idea: GIS represents real-world phenomena digitally.

For vector data, those representations commonly take the form of points, lines and polygons.

WHAT IS DIGITISING? Digitising is the process of creating digital GIS features from real-world objects or existing source material.

For example, you might use recent aerial imagery as a reference and create a GIS feature representing a building, road or parking area.

But before drawing anything, you need to make an important decision:

How should the real-world object be represented?

●

POINT

Represents a location.

Examples: bus stop, tree, hydrant.

━

LINE

Represents a feature with length or a route.

Examples: road, river, footpath.

⬟

POLYGON

Represents an area or boundary.

Examples: building, lake, parking area.

⚠️ Is there always only one correct geometry?

Not necessarily.

The representation can depend on the purpose and scale of the map. A university building may be represented as a polygon on a detailed campus map, but as a point on a small-scale national map.

💡 What You Should Take Away

Digitising is not simply drawing something on a map.

You are making decisions about:

  • what real-world feature should be represented;
  • whether it should be a point, line or polygon;
  • where and how its geometry should be created;
  • which attributes should describe it; and
  • whether the resulting data are reliable enough for their purpose.

Good GIS data begin with good decisions during data creation.
