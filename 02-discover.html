How Does the Real World Become GIS Data?

EXPLORE BEFORE YOU LEARN

Look around your campus.

You can see buildings, roads, parking areas, cafés, trees and many other objects around you.

They exist in the real world. But GIS cannot automatically know about them.

DISCOVERY QUESTION How does something in the real world become GIS data?

☕ A New Café Has Opened

Imagine that a new café has opened on your university campus.

Students know where it is. You can walk to it. You can see the building.

But when you open the university GIS map, the café does not appear.

Go There

Visit the café and record its location using a GPS/GNSS device or mobile application.

CLICK TO DISCOVER
FIELD COLLECTION

🛰️

Look From Above

Find the café on recent aerial or satellite imagery and trace its location or shape.

CLICK TO DISCOVER
DIGITISING

A feature visible on imagery or a map can be traced and represented digitally in GIS.

You will explore this method more closely later in Week 5.
🏠

Use an Address

Perhaps you know the café's address or recognised place name but not its coordinates.

CLICK TO DISCOVER
GEOCODING

GIS can use a location description such as an address to help determine a geographic location.

You will investigate geocoding later in Week 5.
📊

Use Existing Information

The university may already have a spreadsheet containing the café name and coordinates.

CLICK TO DISCOVER
IMPORT & INTEGRATE

Existing tables, coordinates and datasets can be prepared and brought into GIS.

🔗 Wait... What About Data Quality?

In Week 4, you asked whether existing GIS data could be trusted.

Now imagine that you are creating the data.

What could happen if:

  • the GPS position is inaccurate?
  • you trace the café in the wrong location?
  • an address matches the wrong place?
  • the spreadsheet contains incorrect coordinates?

The quality questions from Week 4 have not disappeared. They become even more important when you become the data creator.
